GRAYSCALE ZCASH ETF DRAWS REPORTED $41.1M IN WEEKLY INFLOWS.

Third-party flow data shows a $41.1 million weekly rebound in ZCSH. Grayscale reports $686.2 million in assets, but ETF ownership is not the same as private ZEC self-custody.
By John Doe
OCT 25, 2026 // 4 MIN READ // SOURCE LINKED

WHAT HAPPENED

Grayscale's US-listed Zcash ETF (ZCSH) recorded an estimated $41.1 million in net inflows for the October 19–23 trading week, according to CryptoETF's flow tracker. The reported recovery followed weeks of withdrawals and was driven by four consecutive positive sessions.

CryptoETF lists Monday, October 19 at −$10.4 million, followed by +$1.6 million on Tuesday, +$15.2 million Wednesday, +$14.2 million Thursday and +$20.5 million Friday. Together those rounded daily estimates produce a weekly total of approximately +$41.1 million.

DATA CAVEAT // INDEPENDENT TRACKERS DISAGREE

The $41.1 million is a third-party flow estimate, not an official weekly net-flow figure released by Grayscale. Other trackers differ in their daily attribution. Readers should not treat the figures as audited issuer data.

THE ORIGINAL MARKET REPORT

Rocket Strategy posted the weekly inflow chart on October 24, highlighting the four-day run and the change in direction. The original X post is embedded below.

WHAT GRAYSCALE CONFIRMS

Grayscale's official ZCSH product page reports $686,199,009 in assets under management and 560,771.2934 ZEC held as of October 23, 2026. The fund listed on NYSE Arca on August 25 and charges a 2.50% management fee.

Those holdings establish the fund's size, but they do not independently verify the weekly flow estimate.

WALL STREET EXPOSURE IS NOT FINANCIAL PRIVACY

Zcash supports shielded transactions designed to protect financial information on-chain. Buying ZCSH shares through a brokerage account is different: shareholders gain price exposure through traditional intermediaries but do not control the fund's ZEC, hold its private keys or directly make shielded payments with those shares.

That distinction matters for financial sovereignty. Demand for a regulated investment product does not by itself demonstrate greater use of private peer-to-peer payments.

WHAT TO WATCH

Whether reported inflows persist, whether Grayscale's disclosed holdings continue rising and whether Zcash's privacy features see greater actual use are separate questions. The latest tracker data points to a rebound in investment flows, not yet a definitive shift in long-term adoption.
